Reflection

How did the agents perform on each ticket?

  • 101 — best showing, one blind spot. Inventory saw invoice 501 already covered the size S tee and didn't double-order. But it never questioned that invoice: a $840 "rush reprint" (~105 tees) due 08-28, already 3 days overdue on shop date 08-31. The "Sept 5" promised to Tauhid came from the 5-day lead time, not the real order, so it's a guess.
  • 102 — correct, but short-sighted. Facilities paid the $2,400 Chapel Street rent before the 09-02 due date. It didn't warn that checking is now $1,000 against $1,104 still owed (invoices 501 + 502) and another $2,400 rent on 10-02. Earlier runs flagged this; this one didn't.
  • 103 — good offer, bad purchase timing. The 10% offer ($52.20 × 20 = $1,044, ~$604 gross profit vs. $22 cost) was solid. Reordering 12 hoodies ($264) before the club accepted was a bad policy — that rule came from my boss prompt, not the agent. Bulldog Print's 5-day lead means ordering on acceptance loses almost nothing, and 14 L / 6 XL on the shelf could have covered a size mix with no reorder.

Actual vs. Expected

  • 101: matched, plus an extra Customer Service step to update the customer.
  • 102: matched exactly; the only difference is a human approved the payment, not facilities.
  • 103: right agents and work, wrong order — inventory came in only after Customer Service's shortfall alert. I planned by "who does the work"; the boss planned by "who talks to the customer."

What would have been simpler as one agent with tools?

All three tickets. Each needed 2–5 tool calls in one area (Aisle B stock, one lease, one discount). The team added a boss call per ticket, the 103 alert detour, and an earlier accounting loop on invoice 501. What the team really bought was permission boundaries (customer service can't touch cash), and the code-level spend guard plus human approval gives most of that with one agent.

Three new problems the team could solve with today's tools

  1. Crest Mug restock — 0 on hand in Aisle D; inventory flagged it but no one acted. reorder_stock already routes mugs to Elm City Gifts (3-day lead, $3.50 cost / $14 price).
  2. Move-in week tee restock — Classic Bulldog Tee is at 0 S, 2 XL, 3 L; get_inventory + reorder_stock can top up sizes before they run out.
  3. Pay overdue invoice 501 — pay_invoice with the spend check; $840 is over the $800 limit at $1,000 checking, so it goes to the boss, then a human.

Three new problems it could not solve, and what it would need

  1. October cash crunch ($1,000 vs. $1,104 owed + $2,400 rent on 10-02) — needs a cash-forecast tool and a finance agent that can prioritize or delay bills and request a transfer or payment plan, with human approval.
  2. Delivering orders — QuickShip CT (local courier, 1-day lead) is in the vendors table but no tool uses it. Needs a schedule_delivery tool triggered after receive_stock, so Tauhid's tee actually ships.
  3. Customer replies — the Iron Man messages are never sent, and an "accept" from the Yale AI Club creates nothing. Needs email/inbox tools that send replies (after review) and turn responses into ticket updates, so negotiate_discount(accepted=true) actually runs.
